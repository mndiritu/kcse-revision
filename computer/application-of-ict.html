APPLICATION AREAS OF ICT — WRITING NOTES (KCSE)

1) Introduction

Computers are used in several areas and industries. Many manual jobs have been replaced by computers, making processes faster and more efficient.

Big picture diagram (for memory)

2) Financial systems

Financial systems include:

  • Payroll systems
  • Accounting systems
  • ICT in banks and insurance companies
A) Payroll systems — uses
  • Human Resource Management
  • Salary processing
B) Accounting systems — uses
  • Customer orders entry
  • Billing
  • Inventory management
C) ICT in banks and insurance companies — uses
  • Automated Teller Machines (ATMs)
  • Cheques processing
  • Electronic Funds Transfer
  • Internet banking and mobile banking
  • Asset finance management such as loan processing systems
3) Retail systems (e.g., supermarkets)

ICT in supermarkets — uses

  • Stock control
  • Electronic Point of Sale (EPOS) — barcode reader, Electronic Funds Transfer, acknowledging and appending transactions
  • Security such as CCTV cameras and sensors item tags
  • Advertisements and promotions
4) Reservation systems — uses
  • Hotel booking
  • Airline booking
5) Education — uses
  • Computer Aided Learning (CAL)
  • Computer Aided Teaching (CAT)
  • E-learning
  • Computer-based simulation
  • Virtual learning
  • Record management
  • Exams preparation and processing
6) Industrial systems — uses
  • Computer Aided Design (CAD)
  • Computer Aided Manufacture (CAM)
  • Simulation such as flight simulators
  • Process control using robots
  • Management control (tracking orders, bills and transactions)
  • Competitive tools such as designing new products and services
7) Scientific and research systems — uses
  • Hospitals: control lifesaving machines, diagnosis, intensive care units, health expert systems
  • Weather forecasting: analyse climatic patterns and trends
  • Military: weapons testing, radar systems, launching and guiding missiles, surveillance and tracking target enemies
  • Space exploration: launch space automobiles and monitor flights
  • Nuclear science: design and testing of nuclear weapons
8) Transportation systems — uses
  • Automobile traffic control systems
  • Railway corporations coordinate movement of goods and wagons
  • Shipping control for communication and fleet management efficiently
  • Airline industry controls movement of aircraft, take-off and landing using radar equipment
9) Entertainment systems — uses
  • Business presentations for advertisement purposes
  • Internet, radio and television
  • Music-related equipment such as synthesisers
  • Production of movies and computer games
10) Virtual reality (VR)
A) Uses of virtual reality
  • Entertainment
  • Training military personnel
  • Education
  • Flight simulation
B) Components of Virtual Reality (VR)
  • Gloves: special gloves fitted with sensors that track hand and finger movements
  • Sensors: detect motion, position and orientation of the user’s body for real-time interaction
  • Body suit: wearable suit with sensors to track full-body movements
  • Head-Mounted Display (HMD) headgear: headset that displays the virtual environment in 3D and tracks head movement
  • Virtual reality software: creates and manages the virtual environment (graphics, sound, interaction)

VR components diagram

11) Library system — uses
  • Cataloguing system: locate books and other resources easily
  • Inventory control system: determine books to order or those lost
  • Borrowing and lending system: easier borrowing and returning of materials
  • E-libraries: virtual libraries on the internet offering electronic books and materials
  • Research: using research sites such as Google
  • Security: control theft of books and other library properties
12) Domestic systems — uses
  • Watching movies, playing music and computer games
  • Storing personal information
  • Calculating and storing home budgets
  • Shopping purposes: lists of shopping items and prices; electronic money transfer facilities
  • Internet, radio, televisions, music, games
  • Production of movies and computer games
13) Health expert systems — uses
  • Allows experts with no experience of computers to build and evaluate complex disease models
  • Used to disseminate key findings on research on medical treatment outcomes
14) Office expert systems — uses
  • Assist in decision making process
  • Support planning and scheduling of activities in offices
  • Solve complex problems by reasoning through bodies of knowledge
15) Marketing & Advertising
A) Electronic commerce (E-business)

Transactions are carried out electronically without physical interaction between the seller and the buyer. This saves on travel costs, time and other resources.

B) Electronic presentation
  • Computers are used to create exciting presentations of products using presentation software
  • Computers are used to design catchy presentations using animation software that can be broadcasted, placed on billboards, or on the internet
  • Note: the employee in charge of this work in an organisation is called a webmaster
16) Security (law enforcement agencies) — uses
  • Biometrics: fingerprints, iris, facial, DNA, and general database of outlaws
  • Lie detectors in criminology
  • Forensic analysis
  • Speed up investigations
17) Classroom reality (ordinary resource setting)

How to revise without fancy gadgets

  • For each application area, memorize 3–5 uses (that’s enough to score).
  • Use local examples: supermarket EPOS, mobile banking, school e-learning, traffic control, CCTV.
  • VR question? Don’t panic. Just list uses and components (gloves, sensors, suit, HMD, software).
  • Marketing question? Remember: E-business and electronic presentation + “webmaster”.

The examiner isn’t looking for “creative writing”. They’re looking for you to match the marking scheme.

APPLICATION AREAS OF ICT — QUICK REVISION

Must-master list (KCSE)

  • List the application areas (financial, retail, reservation, education, industrial, research, transport, entertainment, VR, library, domestic, expert systems, marketing, security).
  • Know “uses” for each area (3–6 points each).
  • VR: uses + components.
  • Marketing: e-business + electronic presentation + webmaster.
  • Security: biometrics + forensics + investigations.

1) One-line definitions (safe KCSE wording)

  • Application area of ICT: a field/sector where computers and communication technologies are used to perform tasks and improve efficiency.
  • EPOS: electronic point of sale system used in supermarkets (barcode reader + electronic funds transfer + transactions).
  • E-business: transactions carried out electronically without physical interaction between buyer and seller.
  • Virtual reality: computer-based environment that provides an immersive experience (supported by VR components).
  • Biometrics: identification using body characteristics such as fingerprints, iris, facial features or DNA.

2) Memory prompts (quick practice)

  1. State five application areas of ICT.
  2. Differentiate payroll systems and accounting systems by giving two uses each.
  3. Give five uses of ICT in banks and insurance companies.
  4. List four uses of ICT in supermarkets.
  5. List five uses of ICT in education.
  6. List five uses of ICT in industrial systems.
  7. Give four uses of ICT in transportation systems.
  8. State four uses of ICT in entertainment systems.
  9. State four uses and five components of Virtual Reality.
  10. List four ways ICT is used in security (law enforcement agencies).

3) Exam traps (avoid these)

  • Don’t write “ICT is used everywhere” and stop. KCSE wants area + specific uses.
  • For education, don’t just say “teaching”. Use the terms: CAL, CAT, e-learning, simulation, record management.
  • For retail, include EPOS details (barcode + EFT + transactions) to look serious.
  • For marketing, don’t forget the term webmaster (easy mark).
