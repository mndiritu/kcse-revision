IMPACT OF ICT ON SOCIETY — WRITING NOTES (KCSE)

1) Big picture: what ICT changes in society

Impact means an effect — it can be positive or negative. ICT affects society mainly through:

  • Employment (jobs created, replaced, displaced)
  • Automated production (machines/robots controlled by computers)
  • Workers’ health (RSI, eye strain, stress, isolation, etc.)
  • Environment (power use, heat, radiation concerns, e-waste, toxins)
  • Culture & morals (privacy, integrity, communication habits; crimes)
  • Breakthroughs & future trends (AI + information superhighway)

Fast “mental map” diagram

KCSE move: pick any one “node”, then list positives + negatives, then give an example.

2) Effects on employment

Computers changed how people work. The major effects are:

A) Job creation

Computers created new jobs that did not exist before (especially where computers are widely used).

Examples of places where computers are used

  • Banks and financial institutions
  • Schools and colleges
  • Hospitals
  • Communication companies
  • Reservation and booking systems

Examples of new job titles

  • Computer operators
  • Programmers
  • Network administrators
  • IT / IS managers
  • Database administrators
  • Software developers
  • System analysts
B) Job replacement

Job replacement happens when old jobs disappear and new jobs need higher computer skills.

  • Many clerical/repetitive office jobs are now done by computers.
  • Fewer workers can do the same work (because computers increase speed and accuracy).
  • People who cannot use computers may be replaced by those with computer skills.
C) Job displacement

Job displacement means a worker is moved to another department or position where the new job may not require computer skills. This commonly affects workers who are not willing to learn new computer skills.

How to reduce job losses (KCSE-safe points)

  • Employers organize in-service training.
  • Workers are trained to use new computer systems.
  • This helps workers keep up with changes in ICT.
3) Effects on automated production

Automated production means using machines and computers instead of manual labour to improve speed, quality and accuracy.

Examples of industries using automated production

  • Vehicle assembly plants
  • Oil refineries
  • Food processing factories

In these industries, computers control machines and robots; assembly lines work automatically; production becomes faster and more efficient.

A) Advantages of automated production
  1. Increased efficiency due to balancing workload and production capacity.
  2. Improved customer service because of adequate and high-quality goods.
  3. Faster production (goods produced in time).
  4. Efficient use of resources (raw materials, personnel, equipment).
  5. Less operating expenses.
  6. Reduced accidents in hazardous sectors (e.g. mining, chemical production).
B) Disadvantages of automated production
  1. High initial cost of setting up an automated system.
  2. May lead to unemployment in labour-intensive areas.
  3. Possible lack of compatibility with software and hardware.
  4. Overreliance on automated systems can lead to laziness.
  5. Resistance to change.
C) Why employees may resist automated systems
  • Fear of change: used to old ways of working.
  • Fear of losing jobs: believe computers will replace them.
  • Fear of failure: worry they will not learn/use the new system properly.
  • Loss of control: managers fear computers may reduce their control.
  • Lack of understanding: don’t see how computers will help them.
4) Issues of workers’ health

Some negative effects of ICT on health include:

  • Repetitive Strain Injuries (RSI)
  • Eye strain and headaches (computer vision syndrome)
  • Electromagnetic emissions
  • Stress
  • Isolation
  • Computer chip toxin (for workers in chip factories)
A) RSI (Repetitive Strain Injuries)

RSI are injuries resulting from wrist, hand, arm and muscle strain, plus neck strains due to forced repetitive movement (e.g. entering data using a keyboard).

Remedies for RSI

  • Resting
  • Sitting in a relaxed position
  • Changing typing techniques
B) Eye strain and headaches (Computer Vision Syndrome)

Because computer users keep their eyes at close range with the monitor, they may develop computer vision syndrome (CVS).

CVS symptoms (as listed in notes)

  • Eye strain
  • Headaches
  • Double vision
  • Fatigue

Remedies for CVS / eye strain

  1. Use monitors with good resolution.
  2. Fit monitors with antiglare screens to filter excess light.
  3. Adjust brightness to a comfortable intensity.
  4. Use flat panel screens that emit fewer radiations.
  5. Use good overhead fluorescence tubes.
  6. Avoid flickering monitors and lighting systems.
  7. Have interludes of rest.
  8. Tilt monitors to a convenient position.
C) Ergonomics (workplace design)

Ergonomics means designing the workplace to make work safe, comfortable, and healthy.

Simple “good posture” sketch (for memory)

D) Emissions, stress, isolation, chip toxins
  • Electromagnetic emissions: electronic devices produce small energy waves; use low-emission devices.
  • Stress: pressure to work faster because computers work quickly.
  • Isolation: reduced face-to-face communication can make workers feel lonely.
  • Computer chip toxins: workers in chip factories may be exposed to harmful chemicals.
  • Radiation and VDUs: modern screens do not produce harmful radiation, but long use can still cause eye strain and fatigue.
5) Environmental issues of ICT
A) Energy consumption and radiation concerns

In the past, computers used a lot of electricity, produced a lot of heat, and were believed to emit harmful electromagnetic radiation. Today, modern computers are designed to use less power and produce less heat.

Remedy (named example from notes)

Many devices are now Energy Star compliant, a program started by the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) to encourage low power consumption and protect the environment.

B) E-waste (electronic waste)

Old computers, printers, monitors, ink cartridges and accessories are often thrown away in large amounts. This creates electronic waste which pollutes the environment when dumped in landfills.

Toxic computer waste

Some computer parts contain harmful chemicals. Example from notes:

  • Nickel-cadmium laptop batteries contain toxic cadmium.
  • When dumped in landfills, chemicals can leak into soil and underground water, causing pollution.

6) Cultural effects
A) Moral effects
  • Rapid growth of ICT presents challenges to moral and cultural values.
  • ICT changed the way we talk, affected privacy, human rights and integrity.
  • Exposure to flaming (online messages using derogatory/obscene language).
  • Access to pornographic materials may lead to moral decadence.
  • Free flow of immoral information negatively influences behavior of young and old.
  • Some people use computers to commit vices (e.g. forging certificates, passports and documents) — moral integrity is compromised.
  • On the contrary, ICT can be used as a campaign platform against diseases like AIDS and drug abuse.
B) Computer crimes

Computer crimes involve using computer software for illegal purposes. Examples in the notes include hacking, eavesdropping, piracy, etc.

Types of computer crimes (as listed)

  1. Data manipulation: altering data that enters/exits a computer (e.g. changing data on a certificate).
  2. Time bomb: coding a program to destroy itself after running a certain number of times.
  3. Computer virus: program aimed at destroying/altering data and spreading destruction over a network.
  4. Trap door: creating a special password giving its creator sole access to the system.
  5. Data stealing: stealing information gathered for someone else for illegitimate purpose.
  6. Eavesdropping: tapping without authorization into communication lines carrying computer data/messages.
  7. Industrial espionage: stealing trade secrets (designs, marketing plans) and selling to another company.
  8. Piracy: making unauthorized copies of copyrighted computer software.
  9. Fraud: using computers to cheat others intending to gain money/information.
  10. Sabotage: illegal destruction of data/information to cause loss to an organization.
  11. Logic bomb: program executed when certain conditions are met (e.g. triggered during database updates).
7) Breakthroughs in ICT

ICT has improved many areas, including:

  • Health care
  • Education
  • Communication (e-mail, internet, fax)
  • Research
  • Commerce (imports and exports)
  • Art and design
  • Entertainment (music, videos, games)
  • Transport
8) Evolution & future trends in ICT
A) Rapid evolution in hardware and software
  • Computers are becoming smaller, cheaper, faster and more powerful.
  • Microprocessors can process billions of instructions per second.
  • Devices are becoming smart, portable and more reliable.
  • Software is becoming easier to use and more flexible.
  • Industries are becoming more automated.
B) Artificial Intelligence (AI)

AI is a branch of computer science focusing on making machines behave like humans by learning, reasoning, seeing, hearing, and communicating. AI aims to create smart computers that can think and learn rather than only follow fixed instructions.

Main areas of AI (as listed)
  • Expert systems
  • Natural language processing
  • Voice recognition
  • Voice synthesis
  • Computer vision
  • Artificial neural networks
  • Robotics and perception systems
Expert systems

An expert system is a computer program that works like a human expert in a specific field. Examples of use include medical diagnosis, financial forecasting, locomotive maintenance, and mineral exploration.

Components of an expert system

  1. Knowledge base: stores expert facts and rules.
  2. Inference engine: thinks and gives conclusions.
  3. User interface: allows interaction with the system.

Examples named in notes

MYCIN (medical), DELTA (locomotives), MUDMAN (minerals), STEAMER (navy training), R-1 (system design)

Expert system block diagram

Advantages (as listed)
  • Fast and accurate
  • Stores large amounts of data
  • Gives consistent answers
  • Works 24 hours
  • Can be used remotely
Disadvantages (as listed)
  • Expensive and complex
  • Can make mistakes
  • Cannot explain deeply
  • May replace human experts
Natural Language Processing & speech technologies
  • Natural Language Processing (NLP): enables computers to understand human languages (spoken or written).
  • Voice recognition: users input data by speaking. Challenges: different accents, similar sounding words, background noise.
  • Voice synthesis: computers produce human-like speech.
Computer vision & artificial neural networks
  • Computer vision: computers see and interpret images, text and objects like humans.
  • Artificial neural networks: copy how the human brain works — learn from data, recognize patterns, make predictions. Example in notes: banks use them to detect unusual credit card spending.
Robotics and perception systems

Robots are computer-controlled machines that perform dangerous or difficult tasks. Perception systems allow robots to sense: sight, hearing, touch, smell, taste.

Advantages of robots
  • Work all the time
  • High speed and accuracy
  • High productivity
  • Work in dangerous places
Disadvantages of robots
  • Expensive
  • Replace workers
  • Limited to specific tasks
  • Can malfunction
Laws of robotics (as listed)
  1. A robot must not harm a human.
  2. A robot must obey humans unless it causes harm.
  3. A robot must protect itself unless it conflicts with the first two laws.
C) Expanded information superhighway

This refers to the growth of internet and communication networks. It uses fiber optic and wireless technology, supports faster data transfer and worldwide digital communication. The internet is growing very fast, causing a digital information explosion.

9) Classroom reality: how to revise this topic in an ordinary resource setting

Low-resource revision tricks that still work

  • Employment impacts: use local examples (school, hospital, bank, cybercafé) and link them to job creation/replacement.
  • Automation: watch one short clip (even offline) of an assembly line, then list advantages/disadvantages.
  • Health: do “desk setup” talk — posture + breaks + anti-glare (practical, memorable).
  • Environment: list common e-waste items around you (old phones, chargers, printers) and explain landfill pollution.
  • AI: memorize the AI areas list + expert system components (KCSE loves lists).

KCSE secret: don’t write novels. Write clean bullet points with one short explanation each.

IMPACT OF ICT ON SOCIETY — QUICK REVISION

Must-master lists (KCSE)

  • Main ICT impact areas: employment, automation, health, environment, culture, breakthroughs.
  • Employment effects: job creation, job replacement, job displacement.
  • Automation: definition + advantages + disadvantages + reasons for resistance.
  • Health issues: RSI, CVS/eye strain, emissions, stress, isolation, chip toxins; ergonomics.
  • Environment: energy use/heat; Energy Star; e-waste; toxic waste (cadmium example).
  • Culture: moral effects; computer crimes types.
  • Future trends: hardware/software evolution; AI; information superhighway.
  • AI: areas list + expert system components + laws of robotics.

1) One-line definitions

  • Impact: a positive or negative effect.
  • Automated production: using machines/computers instead of manual labour to improve speed, quality and accuracy.
  • Ergonomics: designing workplace to be safe, comfortable and healthy.
  • Computer crimes: use of computer software for illegal purpose.
  • AI: making machines behave like humans by learning, reasoning, seeing, hearing and communicating.
  • Expert system: program that works like a human expert in a specific field.
  • Information superhighway: growth of internet/communication networks using fiber optic and wireless technology.

2) Memory prompts (short, exam-ready)

  1. State the three effects of ICT on employment and explain each briefly.
  2. Define automated production and give two examples of industries where it is used.
  3. Give four advantages and four disadvantages of automated production.
  4. List five reasons why employees resist automated systems.
  5. Define RSI and give three remedies.
  6. State four symptoms of computer vision syndrome and four remedies.
  7. Explain how e-waste pollutes the environment and give one toxic example from computer waste.
  8. List any six computer crimes and explain two.
  9. Define AI and list the main areas of AI.
  10. Draw/describe the components of an expert system.

3) KCSE traps (don’t donate marks)

  • Don’t confuse job replacement with job displacement — replacement is “old jobs disappear”; displacement is “worker moved to another position.”
  • For computer crimes, give the type + short meaning (e.g., “trap door: special password giving sole access”).
  • For AI, KCSE likes lists — don’t freestyle new AI fields not in your notes.
  • For health issues, always add at least one remedy (RSI/CVS are easy marks).
